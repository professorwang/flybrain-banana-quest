\section*{中文摘要}
本文追问：“在连接组上跑动力学”的仿真结论，有多少是由数据加工选择而非连接组本身决定的？我们用同一个 LIF 内核在两个社区封装的真实连接组（FlyWire FAFB v783：139,255 神经元；MaleCNS v1.0：176,422 神经元）上测量。两个包对谷氨酸的赋号相反（兴奋 vs 抑制），只翻转这一条规则就能双向切换一个全脑高活动态（FlyWire 视叶放电 405,788$\to$0；MaleCNS 2$\to$553,863）。v6 检验这一翻转到底隔离了什么：逐字节等价重建确认变体无误；质量匹配的随机对照（翻转其他递质类别、突触质量不超过谷氨酸规则）同样消除 FlyWire 的高活动态（405,788$\to$1--128），说明该效应在那里不是谷氨酸特异的；在逐组突触质量完全相同时，MaleCNS 的点燃只出现在谷氨酸翻转（279,054 vs 108）。剂量-反应陡峭且有阈值（翻转 25\% 的谷氨酸质量即去掉 FlyWire 98.7\% 的视叶活动；MaleCNS 在 75\%--90\% 之间点燃）。把本项目的 postsynaptic L1 归一化换成每突触固定系数后，符号效应方向不变（Shiu 匹配量级下 22--29 倍），但从全有全无变为梯度，MaleCNS 上谷氨酸的特异性也随之消失甚至反转。参考实现的全局 max 归一化之所以完全不传播，是因为其每突触权重比 Shiu 匹配量级低约 100--170 倍。结论：谷氨酸规则之所以重要，首先因为它是两个包分歧最大的一块突触质量；关于特定递质类别的论断需要质量匹配对照和第二种权重方案。全部结果是模型内的，不能推导真实果蝇生物学；每个数字都可由冻结脚本复现。

\begin{abstract}
\noindent How much of what ``dynamics on a connectome'' simulations show is
set by data-packaging choices rather than by the connectome? We probe one
leaky integrate-and-fire core on two community packages of real fly
connectomes (FlyWire FAFB v783, 139,255 neurons; MaleCNS v1.0, 176,422
neurons). The packages sign glutamate oppositely, and flipping that one rule
switches a whole-brain high-activity regime in both directions (FlyWire
visual-lobe spikes 405,788 $\to$ 0; MaleCNS 2 $\to$ 553,863). We then test
what the flip isolates. Byte-identical rebuilds confirm the variants.
Mass-matched random flips of other transmitter classes also abolish the
FlyWire regime (405,788 $\to$ 1--128), so there it is not glutamate-specific;
at equal per-group synaptic mass, MaleCNS ignites only under glutamatergic
flips (279,054 vs 108). Dose-response is steep: flipping 25\% of the
glutamate mass removes 98.7\% of FlyWire visual activity, and MaleCNS ignites
between 75\% and 90\%. Replacing our postsynaptic L1 normalization by a fixed
per-synapse weight keeps the direction of the sign effect (22--29$\times$ at a
Shiu-matched scale) but makes it graded, and the MaleCNS glutamate
specificity disappears or reverses. The reference global-max normalization, which
propagates nothing, uses per-synapse weights about 100--170$\times$ below that
scale. The glutamate rule matters first because it is the largest block of
synaptic mass on which packages disagree; claims about specific transmitter
classes need mass-matched controls and a second weight scheme. All results
are model-internal and license no inference about fly biology; every number
regenerates from frozen scripts.
\end{abstract}

\section{Background and question}

Banana Quest (电子果蝇·香蕉大作战) is a zero-dependency browser game in which
a leaky integrate-and-fire (LIF) network running on a real connectome helps
drive a 2D foraging fly. The question this note is organized around is not
``does the fly move'' but: \textbf{when a community takes a published
connectome, packages it, and runs off-the-shelf dynamics on it, which
conclusions are set by the packaging choices rather than by the connectome
itself?} Recent work shows that connectivity only partially constrains
dynamics~\cite{beiran2025}; here we ask the practical version of that question
for the browser- and game-scale simulations that now circulate
widely~\cite{awesomefly}. Our testbed is two independent packages of two
different connectomes, probed through one simulation core
(\texttt{src/sim-core.js}, browser worker and Node.js alike):

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{FlyWire FAFB v783}~\cite{zheng2018,dorkenwald2024,schlegel2024}
(139,255 neurons, 2,698,236 aggregated connections, brain only, female),
packaged by snedea/flybrain~\cite{snedea} (MIT) as a 12.4 MB binary with a
63-group annotation; transmitter labels come from synapse-level
prediction~\cite{eckstein2024};
\item \textbf{MaleCNS v1.0}~\cite{berg2026} (176,422 neurons, 6,287,749
connections at $\ge$5 synapses, full male CNS with ventral nerve cord), taken
from fly-brain-minecraft's FLYB v1 bundle~\cite{fbm} (MIT) and converted by
our \texttt{tools/flyb\_to\_bin.py} into the same format with 26 groups and
native-annotation pools.
\end{itemize}

Throughout, \textbf{``the reference implementation'' means snedea/flybrain's
browser simulator}~\cite{snedea}, whose weight scheme we inherited; it is
\emph{not} the model of Shiu et al.~\cite{shiu2024}, which propagates activity
well at its own parameters. The game context matters only as motivation:
connectome spiking participates in steering and feeding gating, \textbf{and}
the game contains explicit motor rules (standby random walk, approach
deceleration and stopping, scripted escape maneuver, wall bounce, scripted
hunger wiring, hand-designed sensor mappings and pool selections). Unlike
embodied models~\cite{wangchen2024}, we simulate no gait.

\subsection{Precursor findings (compressed)}

\textbf{P1 --- the weight scale decides whether anything propagates.} The
reference implementation divides every weight by the global maximum and
multiplies by 0.15. With a heavy-tailed synapse-count distribution (max 2,405
on FlyWire, 2,591 on MaleCNS; median 8), this gives
$6.2\times10^{-5}$ / $5.8\times10^{-5}$ threshold units per synapse. For
orientation, matching Shiu et al.'s parameters (0.275~mV per synapse, 5~ms
synaptic and 20~ms membrane time constants, 7~mV from rest to threshold) to
our discrete core under a 1~tick $\approx$ 1~ms reading gives 0.006
(peak-matched) to 0.010 (time-integral-matched) --- about
\textbf{100--170$\times$ larger}. Unsurprisingly, driving the left olfactory
pool at 0.5 for 100 ticks under global-max produced zero central spikes on
both datasets (100-tick observed maximum voltages 0.780 / 0.977), and even
$10\times$ gain (still 10--17$\times$ below the Shiu-matched scale) produced
only 16 / 131 central spikes. The failure is a scale mismatch, not a property
of either connectome. The game instead uses \textbf{postsynaptic L1
normalization} (each neuron's total absolute input scaled to
\texttt{targetInput}, ``ti''), our engineering choice, whose working point is
dataset-dependent (ti = 3.0 FlyWire / 4.0 MaleCNS).

\textbf{P2 --- coarse bilateral readouts can carry structural bias.} Our
FlyWire descending readout (GNG\_DESC split by soma-x median, 1,791/1,790)
fires the right pool more under \emph{either} unilateral antennal stimulus
(1,704 vs 2,106 left-stim; 1,633 vs 2,133 right-stim): the right pool
carries 24--31\% more spikes, while the left pool's share moves by only
1.4 percentage points between stimulus sides (44.7\% vs 43.4\%). We steer on a
heuristic high-pass readout (deviation from a $\tau = 5$~s EMA baseline),
with no claim that it removes the bias.

\textbf{P3 --- small-seed sweeps overfit.} A 5-seed sweep crowned
\texttt{turnGain} = 1.8 (median first-eat 26.4~s, score 3.00); on 10
out-of-sample seeds it collapsed (8/10, median 57.7~s, score 1.30), worse
than the default on the same seeds (10/10, 49.6~s, 2.70). What improved
performance on the tested seeds was level design (banana spawn ring
150--250~px, median 27.3~s). First-eat statistics count successful episodes
only; seeds 101--110 served both selection and evaluation; the champion's
3.00 requires the historical unbounded spawn distance
(\texttt{"bananaMaxDist":[null]}; 2.20 under today's default).

\section{What the two packagings change}

\textbf{Both are $\ge$5-synapse filtered.} Unsigned aggregation of the
FlyWire source table yields 2,700,513 neuron pairs, none below 5 synapses;
2,277 pairs whose signed weights cancel to exactly zero are dropped, giving
the binary's 2,698,236 edges. The MaleCNS bundle is thresholded at $\ge$5
synapses and drops autapses.

\section{The glutamate sign intervention and its controls}

All probes in this section use L1 normalization, the left olfactory pool at
I = 1.0 for 200 ticks, and report total spikes, the descending group
(FlyWire GNG\_DESC, MaleCNS DN) and the visual group (FlyWire VIS\_ME,
82,318 neurons; MaleCNS VIS\_OL, 89,403 neurons). Runs are deterministic;
randomness enters only through sampling seeds.

\subsection{Matched random controls (E1)}

For every glutamate unit (FlyWire: a presynaptic neuron with $\ge$1 GLUT row,
flipped mass = its GLUT-row synapses; MaleCNS: a glutamatergic neuron,
flipped mass = its output synapses) we draw, without replacement, a control
unit of another transmitter class and flip it in the same direction.
FlyWire controls are neurons labeled ACH/DA/SER/OCT whose excitatory rows
become inhibitory; MaleCNS controls are GABAergic or histaminergic neurons that
become excitatory. \textbf{C-match} pairs within the same functional group and
log$_2$ mass bin; \textbf{C-rand} matches the mass bin only (random
location). Both are then \textbf{mass-capped}: units are removed at random
until the control's flipped synaptic mass does not exceed the glutamate rule's
(per group for C-match, in total for C-rand), so controls can only be weaker.
Ten seeds each; median [min--max]; mass relative to the full glutamate rule.

\subsection{Equal-mass identity test (E1b)}

To separate transmitter identity from mass, we draw a random fraction $\varphi$
of the glutamate units, pair a C-match control to that subset, then trim the
glutamate subset group by group to the control's achieved mass. The two sets
then have (near-)identical per-group masses and differ only in which
neurons they contain. We compare at doses where the response is graded
rather than saturated (ti = 4, ten seeds; G = glutamate subset, C = control).

\subsection{What the intervention does and does not show}

\textbf{Robust across both datasets and both weight schemes:} the packaging
rule for glutamate changes simulated whole-brain activity in a consistent
direction, and a browser package that signs glutamate excitatory (contrary to
Shiu et al.\ and to the MaleCNS package) runs in a different dynamical regime
because of it. \textbf{Not supported:} that glutamate is ``sufficient and
necessary'' in any specific sense. On FlyWire, any inhibitory block of
comparable mass suppresses the regime; the all-or-nothing phenotype is
specific to L1 normalization; and whether glutamatergic neurons are special
beyond their mass depends on dataset and weight scheme (categorical on MaleCNS
under L1, absent or reversed on MaleCNS under linear weights, and moderate on FlyWire
under both, where equal-mass glutamate flips leave 1.2--3.7$\times$ fewer
residual visual spikes). The glutamate rule matters first because it is
the largest block of synaptic mass on which the packages disagree (5.6 million
FlyWire synapses; 14.6 million MaleCNS synapses). \textbf{Boundary:} these are
results about sign conventions inside a point-neuron model; whether glutamate
excites or inhibits at a given synapse in the animal is a physiological
question that no network perturbation here can answer.

\section{Related validation work, and what this note adds}

The fly-brain-minecraft validation record~\cite{fbm} (\texttt{docs/VALIDATION.md})
keeps a silent-brain check, a sugar$\to$MN9 feeding bench reproducing the
published pathway~\cite{shiu2024}, a gain sweep on Shiu's fixed per-synapse
scheme (gain 0.65 chosen, runaway at 0.75), per-table commands and seeds, and
an honestly labeled olfaction limitation (saturating projection neurons, no
clear descending steering signal, a hand-built reflex layer), and marks
absolute firing rates as untrustworthy. flybench~\cite{flybench} offers
pre-registered behavioral tasks on FlyWire v783 and MaleCNS with
shuffled-wiring controls and a reference LIF implementation. Descending and
ascending pathways have been compared across FlyWire, MANC and
FANC~\cite{sturner2025}, and a brain-and-cord connectome (BANC) now
exists~\cite{bates2026}.

\textbf{What this note adds.} (i) An \textbf{audit protocol for packaging
rules}: equivalence-checked rebuilds from source, mass-capped matched random
controls, an equal-mass identity test, dose-response, and a second weight
scheme --- each of which changed or narrowed a conclusion here. (ii) A
concrete, checkable packaging discrepancy (the glutamate sign in a widely
used browser package) and its quantified dynamical consequences. (iii) A scale
diagnosis of the reference global-max failure. (iv) A frozen public pipeline
in which every number regenerates from one command (\S7). Where we converge
with the upstream record --- weak odor$\to$descending steering, a gain cliff
into runaway excitation, per-dataset calibration --- the convergence suggests
model- and packaging-level phenomena rather than demo-specific accidents.

\section{Methodological recommendations}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Publish the sign table, the weight scheme and the effective
weight per synapse}, and run a propagation test; a scheme that is two orders
of magnitude below a physiological scale will look like a silent brain
(\S1.1-P1).
\item \textbf{Before attributing an effect to a transmitter class or a
packaging rule, run mass-matched random controls} (location-matched and
location-random, mass-capped), an equal-mass identity test and a
dose-response (\S3.2--3.4).
\item \textbf{Repeat key results under a second weight scheme}; all-or-nothing
phenotypes can be normalization artifacts (\S3.5).
\item \textbf{Calibrate readout pools against a slow baseline and disclose the
bias} (\S1.1-P2).
\item \textbf{Validate high-variance behavioral metrics out-of-sample}, and
say which seeds selected and which evaluated (\S1.1-P3).
\item \textbf{Disclose explicit motor/game rules}, and \textbf{treat absolute
firing rates as untrustworthy}~\cite{fbm}.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Limitations and open questions}

Point LIF neurons without conductances, synaptic kinetics, delays,
neuromodulation, plasticity or gap junctions; transmitter signs from
prediction pipelines~\cite{eckstein2024}; two individuals of different sex
with different packaging pipelines; no VNC on FlyWire and no gait on either;
explicit game rules (\S1). The controls use one stimulus, 200 ticks and our
own functional groups (63 FlyWire / 26 MaleCNS) with log$_2$ mass bins; units
without a partner in their stratum are left unflipped (FlyWire 9,769 and
MaleCNS 11,230 of the full sets), which the mass cap and the equal-mass test
address but do not remove; the MaleCNS control pool includes histaminergic
photoreceptors. Seeds 101--110 of the game sweeps served both selection and
evaluation (\S1.1-P3). None of this claims biological fidelity, let alone
``uploading''.

Open questions: (1) repeat the sign-rule audit in Shiu et al.'s own model or
flybench's reference implementation, with synaptic kinetics and Poisson input;
(2) which glutamatergic sub-populations carry the MaleCNS identity effect under
L1 normalization; (3) BANC replication~\cite{bates2026}; (4) re-pool the
FlyWire descending readout by native side annotations or named descending
types~\cite{sturner2025} and re-measure the lateral contrast.
